Research on remnant hill prairie connectivity and conservation in the Driftless Region

About This Research

This project investigates the ecological connectivity of remnant hill prairie sites in the Driftless Region, focusing on species distributions, habitat quality, and conservation priorities.

The Driftless Region

The Driftless Region is a unique geological area that escaped glaciation during the last ice age, creating a landscape of steep hills, deep valleys, and diverse ecosystems. Within this region, remnant hill prairies represent some of the rarest and most ecologically significant habitats in the Upper Midwest.

These prairie remnants, found on steep south- and west-facing slopes, harbor specialized plant and animal communities adapted to hot, dry conditions. Many of these sites support species more commonly found in the Great Plains, creating islands of prairie biodiversity within the forested landscape.

Research Objectives

  • Connectivity Analysis: Map and analyze ecological corridors between prairie remnants to understand gene flow and species movement patterns.
  • Species Distribution Mapping: Document plant and animal species distributions across different prairie sites using citizen science data from iNaturalist.
  • Habitat Quality Assessment: Evaluate the condition and conservation value of individual prairie remnants based on species composition and ecological indicators.
  • Conservation Prioritization: Identify high-priority sites for protection and restoration efforts based on connectivity, species richness, and habitat quality.

Methodology

Data Collection

Our research integrates multiple data sources to create a comprehensive picture of hill prairie ecology and connectivity:

  • Field Surveys: On-site botanical and ecological assessments of prairie remnants
  • Citizen Science: Species observations from the iNaturalist platform, particularly the Driftless Remnant Hill Prairies project
  • GIS Analysis: Landscape connectivity modeling using topographic and land cover data
  • Wisconsin DNR Data: Historical imagery and LiDAR elevation data (integration in progress)

Connectivity Modeling

We use landscape resistance modeling to identify potential corridors for species movement between prairie sites. This analysis considers factors such as topography, land cover, habitat suitability, and human disturbance patterns.

Key Findings

Species Diversity

Hill prairie remnants support remarkable plant diversity, with some sites containing over 100 native species in areas smaller than a football field.

Connectivity Challenges

Many prairie sites are highly isolated, with connectivity limited by agricultural land use and forest encroachment.

Conservation Opportunities

Strategic restoration of degraded sites could significantly improve landscape connectivity for prairie species.

Conservation Implications

Our research provides critical information for land managers, conservation organizations, and policymakers working to preserve these unique ecosystems. Key recommendations include:

  • Prioritizing protection of high-connectivity prairie sites
  • Implementing corridor restoration projects to link isolated remnants
  • Managing for species-specific habitat requirements
  • Engaging citizen scientists in ongoing monitoring efforts
